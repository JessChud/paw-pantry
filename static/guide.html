Pet-supply guide

Use these categories to organize a shopping list. Check the current product label and your pet’s size, species, age, and habits before choosing an item. Our starter catalog and retailer links are still being expanded.

Food and treats

Start with the species and life stage. Keep a record of the exact recipe and package size you already use. If your pet has allergies or needs a diet change, discuss the choice with your veterinarian and check the current ingredient list.

Litter and cleaning

Compare litter type, dust, and the size of the tray. For cleaning products, follow the label for surface compatibility, ventilation, and when pets may return to the area. Keep waste bags and other daily essentials on your supply list.

Toys, beds, and enrichment

Match toys to the animal’s size and chewing habits, supervise play, and replace damaged items. Measure the resting space before choosing a bed. Birds, small mammals, fish, and reptiles need species-appropriate enrichment.

Grooming

Choose brushes, combs, nail tools, and shampoos labeled for your animal. Coat type and skin sensitivity matter. Avoid treating a skin or ear problem based on a shopping recommendation.

Leashes, carriers, and travel

Measure for fit and check the manufacturer’s size and weight limits. For air travel, verify the specific airline’s current carrier rules before buying. Add food, water, waste supplies, and any veterinarian-directed care to your packing list.

Bowls and feeding accessories

Consider cleaning, stability, material, and the size of the animal. Record supply use in one consistent unit; a bowl or scoop is not a standardized measure.

Flea-and-tick care

Ask your veterinarian which product is appropriate. Follow the species, age, weight, and use restrictions on the label. Do not select treatment based on a generic pet profile alone.

Fish, birds, small animals, and reptiles

Write down the species and habitat requirements before shopping for feed, substrate, bedding, filters, lighting, or enclosure accessories. Equipment and diets for one species may not suit another.
