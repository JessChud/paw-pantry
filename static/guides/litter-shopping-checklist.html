A litter-shopping checklist for small homes

Paw Pantry · Published September 19, 2026 · Planning guide

Match the litter, tray, carrying weight, and cleanup tools before ordering a refill.

Record the current setup

Start with the litter your cat already uses and the tray’s instructions. Note whether the product is clumping or non-clumping, scented or unscented, and its package size. If you use an automatic litter box, check its manufacturer’s compatibility rules. A broad label such as “cat litter” is not enough to establish that a particular product will work in your box.

Compare packages fairly

Weight and volume describe different things. A lighter package is not automatically a smaller supply, and two materials with the same weight can occupy different volumes. Compare like-for-like products where possible. Track your own refill use over time rather than relying on an assumed number of days printed in promotional copy.

Plan the route from door to tray

Think about stairs, carrying distance, cupboard space, and how you pour a refill. Several smaller bags may be easier to handle than one large package even if the unit cost differs. Measure the storage area and check that the package can be kept according to its instructions. Avoid buying more simply to reach a shipping threshold.

Include the cleanup items

A compatible scoop, a mat that fits the available floor area, and a disposal method are separate decisions. Measure the area around the tray before choosing a mat so that it does not block a door. Follow the litter and local waste-disposal instructions. Keep a refill note when you open a new package; after a few cycles, your own use history will be more informative than a generic estimate.
