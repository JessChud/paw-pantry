Keep a shared supply list without double-counting

Paw Pantry · Published September 19, 2026 · Planning guide

Decide what belongs to one pet and what is shared before calculating refills.

Separate individual from shared supplies

A named pet’s specific food may need its own record, while waste bags or household cleaning supplies may be shared. Assign one record to each physical package or stockpile. Recording the same shared bag independently for two pets makes the household appear to own twice as much as it does.

Add consumption once

For a genuinely shared product, add the actual daily use across the pets who use it. If one uses three units and another uses two, the shared package is depleted by five units per day. This arithmetic does not establish whether pets should share a food or treatment. Keep veterinary instructions and species restrictions separate from inventory calculations.

Use a short naming convention

Include the product, recipe or variant, and storage location. For example, “adult dog food — chicken recipe — kitchen bin” is easier to distinguish from “cat food — salmon recipe — utility cupboard” than two records called “food.” Keep the original package information available so a shorthand label does not replace the ingredient or usage instructions.

Review exceptions

When a pet changes diet, a guest animal stays over, or a second package is opened, update the record rather than stacking estimates on top of the old one. Once a week, compare the list with what is actually present. Paw Pantry’s current private API is a single-owner workspace; unrelated households should not share its credential or records.
