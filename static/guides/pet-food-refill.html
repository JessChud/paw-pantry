How to work out when a bag of pet food will run out

Paw Pantry · Published September 19, 2026 · Planning guide

A practical way to turn package size and actual household use into a refill date.

Start with the amount you actually have

A package label tells you what you bought, not what remains. If a bag is already open, estimate or weigh the remaining food and start your calculation from today. If you are recording a new bag, use the opening date. Recording a purchase date weeks before you start using the bag will make the estimate too early.

Keep both numbers in the same unit

Divide the remaining amount by the amount your household uses each day. For example, 80 ounces used at 2.5 ounces per day lasts 32 days. This is an arithmetic example, not a feeding recommendation. Do not divide a weight in pounds by a scoop count: the scoop needs a reliable weight measurement first. Add together the daily amounts for pets sharing the same bag.

Build in time to shop

Choose a lead time that reflects your shopping schedule and the delivery estimate shown at checkout. A 32-day supply with a five-day lead time means checking the bag and considering a reorder after 27 days. Recalculate when portions change, another pet starts sharing the food, or you notice the original estimate was inaccurate.

Keep a small record

Record the recipe, bag size, date opened, amount remaining, daily use, and unit in one place. Our calculator below produces an estimate in your browser. It does not choose a portion size, send a reminder, or buy food. For a diet or portion change, use the package directions and your veterinarian’s advice rather than this calculation.
